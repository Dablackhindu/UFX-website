Digital twin · off-plan & previz

The apartment is built.
The view is measured.

An off-plan buyer is asked to take two things on trust at once: that the finish will look like that, and that the outlook will be like that. We build the first in Unreal and capture the second for real — so only one of them is still a promise.

01 / Built

The apartment, in Unreal

Real-time, walkable, properly lit — the unbuilt flat standing up as software rather than as a sequence of stills. A buyer moves through it at their own pace instead of being shown the three angles that photograph best.

Because it is a build and not a render, a specification change is a change to the build. One scheme can carry every finish level and every orientation, rather than one frozen impression per apartment type.

02 / Captured

The context, measured

The site, the neighbouring buildings and the outlook are captured from reality — and the view is taken from the true height of that floor, not from an artist's estimate of it.

So the buyer on the fourteenth floor is not looking at an impression of what they will see. They are looking at what is there, measured, from where they will be standing.

Both halves exist. Separately.

A CGI studio will model the apartment beautifully. A survey firm will scan the site accurately. Both are good at their half — and a buyer is still asked to join the two together in their head, which is exactly the point at which they hedge.

01 / The gap

Renders sell the promise

They do it well, which is why you commission them. What they cannot do is verify themselves — and the outlook is the part a buyer most wants verified.

02 / Distance

The deposit comes from abroad

A buyer who cannot stand on the plot commits on media alone. Give them something that can be interrogated and the decision stops needing a flight.

03 / Record

A capture is dated evidence

Because the context is captured rather than modelled, the view is a record. If a buyer later argues the outlook wasn't what they were shown, that is already settled.

Proof · the capture half

Kings Cross Residence

A three-floor London home, captured in full and rebuilt as a real-time twin. Residential rather than off-plan — but it is the same instrument, and it shows the thing a render cannot fake: the measured geometry sitting underneath the picture.

Hold the clip — or tap, on a phone — to drop to the point cloud

Every surface in that clip has a real dimension behind it. That is what separates a capture from a visualisation, and it is why the questions that normally force a second site visit — clearance, fit-out, sightlines — can be answered off the model.

For a scheme, the same instrument goes on the plot and the streets around it. The interior is built; everything the interior looks out at is recorded.

Proof · the real-time half, at scale

Casepak

An industrial recycling facility captured with the XGRIDS Portal Cam at roughly 200,000 drone and LiDAR images, processed to Gaussian splats, optimised through Volinga and deployed in Unreal as a standalone application.

It is not a showreel piece. The business runs it as its live training, maintenance and planning environment, with data overlays on the machinery. A terabyte-scale dataset holding 60+ FPS at 4K is a harder real-time problem than a residential scheme — which is rather the point of citing it.
